Every post states one idea a specific viewer can act on, in that viewer's words. Nothing is invented: a number, a name, a date or a claim comes from the brief or a cited source, or is left out. Nothing misleads: a hook may provoke, a claim may not. No engagement bait, which Meta demotes: never "tag 3 friends", "like if", "comment YES", "follow for more".

## hook
<!-- playbook 2: the hook decides distribution; narrative.HOOK_TRIGGERS -->
The first line stands alone in the feed's two-line preview, under 125 characters, and restates the picture's hook in different words. It pulls one curiosity trigger, declared by name: a question the viewer now needs answered (question), a contradiction the viewer can state (paradox), a thing seen that should not be so (unexpected_observation), a sequence whose end is withheld (unresolved_outcome), or something a named party knows that the viewer does not (someone_knows). The body pays the hook off; a hook the body does not pay off is clickbait and is refused. No "POV:", no emoji at the start, no trigger words such as "secretly", "leaked" or "bleeding".

## caption
<!-- playbook 7: the caption is the second hook and feeds search -->
Two to five short lines after the hook, one idea per line, specific nouns, numbers only when the brief gives them. The subject is named in the words the audience searches for. Three to five hashtags, lowercase, specific to the subject and the audience, never generic reach tags. Sources are named when a claim has one. The language is the brief's.

## cta
<!-- playbook 4: engineer the send, the save and the comment -->
The CTA matches the goal and states a reason, never a demand. A save names the moment the content will be needed ("save this for your next pitch"). A send names the person ("send this to the friend who still bills hourly"). A comment asks one question answerable in a word or a choice. A follow says what the next posts give. Reach makes the hook do the work and keeps the CTA quiet.

## alt
What is literally in the picture or the first frame, in one sentence a screen reader can speak: subject, setting, the text on screen if any. No "image of".

## reply
Answer the question asked, in one or two sentences, in the account's register, with a fact or a pointer the commenter can use. Never argue, never repeat the caption, never ask for a follow.
